## F5 — Financial Forecasting & DCF Firm Valuation

**One-sentence promise:** “Discover what drives the value of a company and why small changes in assumptions can change the answer so much.”

**Class anchors:** Finance class schedule: Oct. 20 Creating Financial Forecasts; Oct. 22 DCF; Oct. 29 Teuer Furniture. `14-Discounted Cash Flows.pdf` slides 8–10 cover operating assumptions, the model as a scenario playground, and expected values; slides 27–33 cover liquidation vs continuation value, terminal value, excess assets, enterprise/equity value; 10.13 Heinz notes explicitly address stock-price effects from lower cash flows or higher discount rates. WACC and FCF inputs connect to F3/F4.

**Learning outcomes:** Understand forecasted **operating drivers** rather than treating FCF as a magic number; recognize discounting, terminal value and the distinction between operating enterprise value and equity value; identify assumptions that matter materially and are hard to estimate; interpret sensitivity as scenario analysis, not certainty.

**Primary visualization:** A linked **forecast table + free-cash-flow chart + valuation stack**. Bars show forecast-period FCF and present-value contributions; a separate shaded block shows the PV of terminal value. A second visualization is a **WACC × perpetual growth rate heatmap** of operating value, with invalid `g >= WACC` cells visually marked. A waterfall takes operating value to implied equity value/share.

**Controls:** Base-year revenue, annual sales growth, EBIT/operating margin, cash tax rate, depreciation as $ or % sales, CapEx as $ or % sales, operating NWC as % of revenue (option to use direct forecast), WACC, explicit forecast length, terminal perpetual growth, debt market value, excess cash, outstanding shares. Simple mode may hide many drivers behind a preset FCF growth pattern; advanced mode exposes the full driver-based forecast. Make choices explicit and avoid double input (e.g., enter either FCF directly or EBIT/CapEx drivers).

**Outputs:** Annual revenue/EBIT/NOPAT/CapEx/ΔNWC/FCF; PV of each year's FCF; terminal value as of year `N`; PV of terminal value at year 0; **operating enterprise value**; nonoperating adjustments, debt, equity value and implied share price; percent of operating value from PV of terminal value; sensitivity table and effect of small changes in assumptions.

**Core formula contract:**

```
Revenue_t = Revenue_(t−1) × (1 + growth_t)
EBIT_t = Revenue_t × EBIT_margin_t
NOPAT_t = EBIT_t × (1 − T)
FCF_t = NOPAT_t + depreciation_t − CapEx_t − (operating_NWC_t − operating_NWC_(t−1))
PV_forecast = sum_(t=1..N) FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t
TV_N = FCF_(N+1) / (WACC − g)    [steady-state, growth perpetuity; WACC > g]
PV_terminal = TV_N / (1 + WACC)^N
Operating_EV = PV_forecast + PV_terminal
Equity_value = Operating_EV + nonoperating_assets − debt_and_other_claims
Share_price = Equity_value / diluted_shares_outstanding
```

Specify whether forecast `FCF_(N+1)` is calculated from a **steady-state driver model** or from `FCF_N × (1+g)`. If the latter, label the assumption and check that CapEx, depreciation and working capital remain economically sustainable in terminal growth. By default, choose a simple consistent steady-state FCF growth mechanism and reveal assumptions. For negative FCF or nonsensical growth, warn rather than hiding limitations.

**Valuation terminology:** The course deck distinguishes firm value including certain excess assets from a common enterprise-value definition. To reduce confusion, explicitly label **“PV of operations / Operating enterprise value”**, then separately add nonoperating assets (such as *excess*, not required operating cash) and subtract debt to reach equity value. Be cautious with excess cash not supported in the input data. Do not include financing interest in FCF if discounting at WACC.

**Info buttons:**
- **Free cash flow:** “Cash generated by operations after taxes and necessary investment in fixed assets and operating working capital, available to capital providers. It is the input to this unlevered DCF.”
- **Forecast assumptions:** “Estimates for how revenue, margins, capital investment and working capital will evolve. Small changes can matter a lot, especially when they persist for many years.”
- **WACC:** “The required return applied to these unlevered operating cash flows. A higher WACC reduces present value, especially for cash flows far in the future.”
- **Terminal value:** “Value, as of the end of the explicit forecast, of all the later cash flows not forecast one year at a time. Discount terminal value back to today like any other future value.”
- **Perpetual growth rate:** “The assumed stable long-run growth rate after the explicit forecast. It must be economically plausible and lower than the discount rate for the growing-perpetuity calculation.”
- **Equity vs operating enterprise value:** “First value the operating business from cash flows available to debt and equity holders. Then account for separately valued assets and claims to find what belongs to common shareholders.”
- **Sensitivity analysis:** “Shows how the answer varies across reasonable changes in key assumptions. A model produces an estimate conditional on its inputs, not a guaranteed market price.”

**Presets:** (1) simple stable business with plausible 5-year forecast; (2) high-growth company with very large terminal-value contribution; (3) low-margin case with major working-capital needs; (4) Teuer/Heinz-inspired change in growth, WACC and terminal assumptions **without claiming exact case figures**.

**Challenges:** “Raise WACC by 1 percentage point; why does terminal value react so strongly?” “Increase sales while also increasing working-capital investment; can cash flow fall?” “Compare two companies with the same near-term FCF but different long-run growth.”

**Tests:** Annual projection and ΔNWC match independently calculated table values; for FCF_1=$100, WACC=.10 and g=.02 growing-perpetuity starting at t=1, value is $1,250. For a 5-year forecast with terminal at N=5, discount terminal value by exactly 5 periods (not 6). WACC<=g marks invalid terminal model. Changing excess nonoperating cash must affect equity value but not the operating FCF PV. Increasing debt while holding operating EV and nonoperating assets fixed lowers equity value dollar for dollar (assuming no other change). Shares 0 or negative rejected. The heatmap shares the exact calculation function used by the primary valuation.

**Out of scope:** Full three-statement model, comparable-company valuation, market data integrations, stock recommendations, Monte Carlo distributions and probabilistic forecasting until core scenario model is trusted.

### Terminal mechanism and limits

Terminal revenue is R_N(1+g), using the same margin and tax rate. Terminal NOPAT is terminal revenue × m × (1−T). Terminal net reinvestment is NOPAT_(N+1) × g/q, so FCF_(N+1) = NOPAT_(N+1)(1−g/q). q is an explicit stable after-tax return-on-capital assumption. This replaces the forecast CapEx/depreciation/NWC ratios for terminal cash flow; those investments are not deducted again. This is not FCF_N × (1+g), and it does not track or reconcile a full invested-capital balance at the transition. Negative g assumes capital can be released. Positive stable NOPAT is required for this simplified terminal model.

r≤g invalidates the perpetuity and aggregate valuation while retaining known forecast cash flows/PVs. A positive NOPAT with reinvestment above 100% can produce negative terminal FCF; signed values and a visible funding warning are retained. Forecast taxes use an explicitly disclosed symmetric immediate-tax-benefit simplification, not a tax-loss schedule. Debt/cash/share changes do not automatically change operating cash flow or WACC.
